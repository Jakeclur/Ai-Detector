How to change a flat tyre

Pull over somewhere safe and flat, away from traffic. Turn on your hazard lights and put the handbrake on. If you have passengers, get them out of the car and away from the road.

Find your spare tyre, jack and wheel brace. These are usually under the floor in the boot. Check your car's manual if you can't find them.

Loosen the wheel nuts before you lift the car. Use the wheel brace to turn each nut anticlockwise about half a turn. They might be tight, so you may need to use your foot or body weight. Don't take them off yet.

Place the jack under the car's jacking point, which is usually a reinforced spot just behind the front wheel or in front of the back wheel. Your manual will show you exactly where. Raise the car until the flat tyre is about 5cm off the ground.

Remove the wheel nuts completely and pull the flat tyre off. Keep the nuts somewhere they won't roll away.

Line up the spare tyre with the wheel studs and push it on. Screw the nuts back on by hand as far as they'll go.

Lower the car until the tyre touches the ground, then tighten the nuts with the wheel brace in a star pattern, rather than going around in a circle. This helps the wheel sit evenly.

Lower the car completely and remove the jack. Give each nut one last firm tighten.

Many spare tyres are only meant for short distances at low speeds, so get your flat repaired or replaced as soon as possible.

Should students be allowed to use AI for homework?

Students should be allowed to use AI for some homework, but schools need clear rules about how it can be used.

AI tools can be genuinely helpful for learning. A student who doesn't understand a maths concept can ask for it to be explained in a different way, at any time of night, without feeling embarrassed. AI can quiz students before a test, suggest how to structure an essay or point out where an argument is weak. Used like this, it works more like a patient tutor than a shortcut.

Banning AI completely is also unrealistic. The tools are free, easy to access and getting better every month. Students will use them whether they're allowed to or not. Teaching them how to use AI responsibly is more useful than pretending it doesn't exist, especially because most of them will use these tools in their future jobs.

However, there are real risks. If a student asks AI to write their whole essay, they skip the thinking that homework is supposed to build. Writing helps people organise their ideas, and practice problems build skills through repetition. AI can also give confident but wrong answers, and students who rely on it may not notice.

The best approach is for schools to set clear boundaries. AI could be allowed for brainstorming, explanations and feedback, but not for producing final answers. Teachers could also design more tasks that are done in class or that ask students to explain their thinking.

AI isn't going away, so the goal should be to help students use it to learn more, not less.

Why do cats purr?

Cats purr for more than one reason, and scientists still don't fully understand it.

The most familiar reason is contentment. Many cats purr when they're being stroked, curled up somewhere warm or settling down to sleep. In these situations, purring seems to signal that the cat feels relaxed and safe.

But cats also purr when they're stressed, injured or unwell. Some purr at the vet or while giving birth. This suggests purring isn't just a sign of happiness. It may also be a way for cats to calm themselves, a bit like how people might hum or rock when they're anxious.

Kittens can purr when they're only a few days old. They purr while feeding, which may help their mother know they're nearby and doing well. Mother cats often purr back, which may help the kittens find her, since they're born with their eyes closed.

Some cats also use a special "solicitation purr" when they want food. Research has found that this purr includes a higher-pitched sound, a bit like a baby's cry, which humans find hard to ignore. If you've ever been woken up by a purring cat at 5am, that might be why.

As for how they do it, purring happens when the muscles in a cat's voice box twitch rapidly, about 25 to 150 times a second. This makes the vocal folds vibrate as the cat breathes in and out.

There's also some evidence that the frequency of purring may help bones and tissues heal, but this hasn't been proven.

What's the difference between a virus and bacteria?

Viruses and bacteria can both make you sick, but they're very different things.

Bacteria are single-celled living organisms. Each one is a complete cell that can take in food, produce energy and reproduce on its own by splitting in two. They're found almost everywhere, in soil, water and inside your body. Most bacteria are harmless, and many are helpful. The bacteria in your gut help you digest food and protect you from harmful germs. Only a small number cause illnesses, such as strep throat, food poisoning and tuberculosis.

Viruses are much smaller, often around a hundred times smaller than bacteria. They aren't made of cells and can't do anything on their own. A virus is basically a piece of genetic material wrapped in a protein coat. To reproduce, it has to get inside a living cell and hijack it, forcing the cell to make copies of the virus. Colds, the flu, COVID-19 and chickenpox are all caused by viruses.

This difference matters for treatment. Antibiotics kill bacteria by attacking parts of their cells, such as their cell walls. Viruses don't have those parts, so antibiotics don't work on them. This is why doctors won't prescribe antibiotics for a cold. Taking them when you don't need them can also help bacteria become resistant.

Viral infections are usually treated by managing symptoms while your immune system fights them off. Some, like the flu, can also be treated with antiviral medicines, and many can be prevented with vaccines.

How do I stop procrastinating?

Make the first step tiny. Procrastination is often less about laziness and more about avoiding a task that feels big, boring or uncomfortable. Instead of "write my history essay," your task becomes "open the document and write one sentence." Once you start, it's usually easier to keep going.

Work out why you're avoiding it. Are you confused about what to do? Worried it won't be good enough? Just bored? Each one needs a different fix. If you're confused, ask for help. If you're worried about quality, give yourself permission to write a bad first draft. If you're bored, pair the task with music or a reward.

Use a timer. Set it for 20 or 25 minutes and promise yourself you'll work until it goes off. Then take a short break. Knowing there's an end point makes it easier to begin.

Remove distractions before you start. Put your phone in another room, not just face down on the desk. Close tabs you don't need. It's much easier to resist temptation when it isn't within reach.

Set deadlines that come before the real deadline. If an assignment is due on Friday, aim to finish your draft by Wednesday.

Be kind to yourself when you slip up. Feeling guilty about procrastinating often leads to more procrastinating. Notice it, then just start again.

Everyone procrastinates sometimes. The goal isn't to never do it, but to catch it earlier and get back on track faster.

Explain the water cycle.

The water cycle is the continuous movement of water around the Earth, between the oceans, the air and the land. It's powered by energy from the sun and by gravity.

It starts with evaporation. The sun heats water in oceans, rivers and lakes, turning some of it into water vapour, which rises into the air. Plants also release water vapour through tiny holes in their leaves, a process called transpiration.

As the water vapour rises, it cools. Cooler air can't hold as much moisture, so the vapour condenses into tiny droplets of water or ice crystals. These gather together to form clouds. This stage is called condensation.

When the droplets in a cloud join together and become too heavy to stay in the air, they fall back to the ground as precipitation. This can be rain, snow, sleet or hail, depending on the temperature.

Once the water reaches the ground, it can go in several directions. Some flows over the surface as runoff, into creeks and rivers and eventually back to the ocean. Some soaks into the soil and becomes groundwater, which can stay underground for a very long time. Some is taken up by plants or frozen in glaciers and ice caps.

Eventually, the water evaporates again, and the cycle continues.

The water cycle matters because it moves fresh water around the planet. It fills our rivers and dams, waters crops and shapes the weather. Changes in temperature can speed it up or slow it down, which is one reason climate change affects rainfall and droughts.

Is it better to rent or buy a house?

It depends on your situation, and neither option is always better.

Buying a home has some big advantages. Each mortgage repayment builds equity, meaning you gradually own more of the property. Over the long term, property values in most Australian cities have gone up, so many owners have ended up with a valuable asset. Owning also gives you security, since you can't be asked to leave when a lease ends, and you can renovate or keep pets without asking permission.

But buying is expensive and risky. You need a deposit, which is often 10 to 20 per cent of the price, plus stamp duty, legal fees and inspections. Once you own a home, you're responsible for repairs, council rates and insurance. If interest rates go up, your repayments can rise sharply. And property prices don't always increase, especially over short periods.

Renting is more flexible. You can move for a new job or a change in lifestyle without having to sell. Upfront costs are much lower, and the landlord pays for most repairs. If you invest the money you would have spent on a deposit and higher costs, you can still build wealth, although this takes discipline.

The downside of renting is that you have less control and less security. Rents can rise each year, and you might have to move when your lease ends.

In general, buying makes more sense if you plan to stay in one place for a long time and have stable income. Renting can be smarter if you value flexibility or are still working out where you want to live.
