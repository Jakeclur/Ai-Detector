Vaccines basically train your immune system to recognise a germ before you actually catch it. When a virus gets into your body it has these molecules on the outside called antigens, and your immune cells spot them and make antibodies that stick to them. They also keep memory cells so next time they can react way faster. The first time is slow which is why you get sick, but the second time your body fights it off in a few days or even hours before you notice anything.

A vaccine gives your body that first time without the danger. Some vaccines have a weakened or dead version of the germ, some just have one part of it like a surface protein, and mRNA vaccines (like the covid ones) give your cells instructions to make the protein for a little while. None of them can give you the actual disease but your body still learns to make the antibodies.

Thats also why you need boosters for some vaccines, because the memory can wear off. When enough people are vaccinated the germ cant spread as easily, which protects people who cant get vaccinated like babies or people with weak immune systems. My little sister got her vaccines last month and she was fine apart from a sore arm.

Photosynthesis is the process plants use to make food (glucose) from light. The word equation is carbon dioxide + water -> glucose + oxygen, and it needs light energy.

It takes place in the chloroplasts which are mostly in leaf cells. Chloroplasts have chlorophyll in them, a green pigment that absorbs red and blue light but reflects green light, so that's why leaves look green to us.

There's two stages. In the light dependent reactions, which happen in the thylakoid membranes, the chlorophyll absorbs light and uses the energy to split water. This makes oxygen which the plant releases, and it also makes ATP and NADPH. Then in the light independent reactions (the Calvin cycle) in the stroma, the plant uses the ATP and NADPH to turn CO2 into glucose.

The glucose gets used for respiration, stored as starch or made into cellulose for the cell walls. In our prac we tested leaves for starch with iodine and the ones that were in the dark the whole time didn't go blue-black, which shows they couldn't photosynthesise.

The rate depends on light intensity, CO2 concentration and temperature. Whichever one is lowest limits the rate even if theres heaps of the others, these are called limiting factors.

World War One was caused by four main long term factors which are militarism, alliances, imperialism and nationalism (MAIN). Before 1914 the big European countries were building up huge armies and navies, especially Britain and Germany who were in a naval race, and this made them really suspicious of each other. Europe was also split into two alliances, the Triple Entente (Britain, France and Russia) and the Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy), so if two countries got in a fight everyone else would get dragged in. Countries were also competing for colonies in Africa and Asia, and nationalism was causing tension in the Balkans where Slavic people wanted to be independant from Austria-Hungary. The short term cause was the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand on 28 June 1914 by Gavrilo Princip, a Bosnian Serb, in Sarajevo. Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia and gave them an ultimatum with Germany backing them up, and because of the alliances it turned into a huge war within a few weeks. In my opinion the alliances were the most important cause because without them it would have just been a small war between two countries.

In To Kill a Mockingbird Harper Lee shows that courage isn't about being strong or winning, it's about doing the right thing even when you know you'll probably lose.

The best example of this is Atticus defending Tom Robinson. He knows the jury is all white and will most likely find Tom guilty, and he knows people in Maycomb will turn on his family for it. But he does it anyway, and tells Scout that if he didn't he "couldn't hold up my head in town." So for Atticus being brave means following his conscience no matter what happens.

Mrs Dubose is another example. Jem thinks she's just a mean old lady but after she dies Atticus tells him she was trying to beat her morphine addiction before she died. He says real courage is "when you know you're licked before you begin but you begin anyway and you see it through no matter what." I think this changes how Jem sees bravery because before that he thought being brave meant having a gun.

This links back to when Atticus shoots the mad dog. Jem and Scout think it's amazing but Atticus doesn't act like it's a big deal. Lee puts these two things close together to show that the quiet kind of bravery is actually harder and more important.

The gold rushes in the 1850s changed Australia a lot. Gold was found near Bathurst in 1851 and then at Ballarat and Bendigo, and people came from everywhere, Britain, Ireland, America, Europe and China. The population basically tripled in ten years, from around 430,000 to over 1.1 million. Melbourne went from a small town to one of the richest cities in the British empire.

It also made the colonies rich. The money paid for railways, the telegraph, buildings and universities. Wages went up too, and lots of workers left farms to go to the goldfields.

Politics changed as well. The miners hated paying for the expensive licence and the way the police checked them, and in 1854 it led to the Eureka Stockade at Ballarat. The rebels lost but after it miners got the vote and Victoria got things like the secret ballot. We went to Sovereign Hill on our year 9 camp and they had a whole show about Eureka which was pretty cool.

But there were bad parts too. Chinese miners were attacked, for example in the Lambing Flat riots, and there were laws against them that later led to the White Australia policy. Aboriginal people lost even more of their land and their water and food sources got wrecked by the miners.

Leo's new school was twice as big as his old one and all the corridors looked exactly the same. He stood outside the office holding his timetable, trying to figure out where B14 was. He'd walked past B12 and B16 twice already. Everyone else was walking around in groups laughing about stuff he didn't know about.

"You look lost," a girl said. She had a backpack covered in badges.

"Im trying to find B14."

"Thats my maths class, its upstairs. They numbered them weird." She started walking and he followed her. "Im Priya."

Maths was ok. The teacher made him stand up and say something about himself and he panicked and said he had a lizard. A boy at the back laughed. At recess Leo sat on a bench pretending to read his timetable so it didnt look like he had nowhere to go.

Then Priya walked past with her friends and came back. "What kind of lizard?"

"A bearded dragon. Hes called Kevin."

The boy who laughed was one of her friends, Sam, and it turned out he had a bearded dragon too called Steve. They argued about which name was worse for the whole of recess.

At the end of the day Leo still didnt know where most of his classes were but at least he knew where to sit at lunch.
