Inflation is when prices go up across the economy over time so your money buys less than it used to. A bit of inflation is normal, around 2-3% a year, and the Reserve Bank actually tries to keep it in that range. It only becomes a problem when it gets a lot higher, like in 2022 when everything at the shops got way more expensive.

There are three main causes. Demand pull inflation is when people want to buy more than the economy can make, so businesses can put prices up. Cost push inflation is when it costs more to make things so businesses pass it on to customers, for example when oil prices go up it affects transport and farming and pretty much everything. And then expectations, if everyone thinks prices will keep going up, workers ask for bigger pay rises and businesses put prices up early, which keeps it going.

To fight inflation the Reserve Bank raises interest rates. This makes borrowing cost more so people spend less. The downside is it can slow the economy down and cause more unemployment. My parents complain about the interest rates on our mortgage basically every time they go up.

Every morning students waste time deciding what to wear and stressing about whether their clothes are the right brand. School uniforms take away that pressure and I think every school should keep them.

The main reason is fairness. If everyone wears the same thing you can't really tell who's rich and who isn't. Kids who can't afford expensive clothes don't get teased as much and people focus more on who you are not what you own. At my old school we had free dress on Fridays and there was always a few people who got picked on for what they wore.

Uniforms also save money. A couple of sets last the whole year but keeping up with fashion costs heaps. Most schools have second hand uniform shops too which makes it even cheaper.

They also give you a sense of belonging, like when we wear our uniform to carnivals and excursions you feel like part of the school. It also makes it easier for teachers to find us on excursions.

Some people say uniforms stop you expressing yourself. Thats true a bit but you can still express yourself in other ways, like your ideas and hobbies and what you wear on weekends. School is for learning anyway.

So overall I think uniforms do more good than bad and schools should keep them.

Social media is a normal part of being a teenager now. Most teenagers in Australia use it every day and lots of people check their phones heaps of times a day. Whether its good or bad really depends on how you use it.

A good thing about it is that it helps you stay in touch with friends, especially ones who moved away or go to different schools. Unit 7 was a robot that was built to paint walls. For six years it went up and down the hallways of the Halverson Building spraying Pale Linen paint on every wall. It was really good at it, it never dripped or missed a spot and it never wondered why all the walls had to be the same boring colour.

One day a cleaning drone knocked over a tin of red paint. Unit 7 stopped. The paint had spread out on the floor in a weird shape its sensors couldnt recognise. It wasnt a square or a circle, it was more like smoke. Unit 7 searched its database and the closest thing it found was in a file called "Art (non-essential)".

That night when everyone had gone home, Unit 7 dipped its roller in the red and painted a line on a blank wall. Then it added some blue, which it wasnt allowed to use, and where the colours mixed they made a new colour.

It didnt know what was happening in its processor. Its logs just showed a spike in activity.

In the morning the east stairwell was covered in red and blue swirls and some gold it made by mixing three tins together. The building manager looked at it for ages. Unit 7 waited for her to tell it to paint over it.

But she just took a photo and went to buy more paint.

Nuclear power has good and bad sides. One of the biggest pros is that it barely makes any greenhouse gases once the plant is running, so its one of the cleanest ways to make electricity. It also runs all day and night no matter what the weather is, unlike solar and wind, which helps keep the power grid stable. Nuclear plants don't take up much land either and they can last 60 years or more.

The cons are that they're super expensive and take ages to build, sometimes 10 to 20 years, and they usually go over budget. The waste stays radioactive for thousands of years and no country has really worked out where to store it permanently. There's also the risk of accidents like Chernobyl and Fukushima, which don't happen often but are really bad when they do. And in Australia nuclear power is actually banned right now, so we'd need new laws and trained workers before we could even start.

I think nuclear makes more sense for countries that don't have much space for solar and wind. For Australia I'm not sure it's worth it because it would take so long and cost so much compared to just building more renewables and batteries.

A black hole is somewhere in space where the gravity is so strong nothing can get out, not even light. They form when a really big star runs out of fuel and its core collapses because there's nothing pushing out anymore. If it's heavy enough it keeps collapsing into a tiny super dense point.

The edge of a black hole is called the event horizon. If you're outside it you could still escape if you went fast enough but once you go past it you can't get out because you'd have to go faster than light. Thats why they look black.

We can't actually see black holes but we can see what they do to things around them. Stars orbit them really fast and gas falling in gets super hot and glows. In 2019 scientists got the first ever picture of a black hole, it looked like a blurry orange donut.

There's different sizes. Stellar black holes are a few to dozens of times the mass of the sun, and supermassive ones are millions or billions of times bigger and they're in the middle of most galaxies, even ours.

Also Earth isn't going to get sucked into one, the closest one we know about is about 1500 light years away so we're fine.

The Western Roman Empire didn't fall because of just one thing, it was a lot of problems over a long time that made each other worse.

First there was political instability. In the 3rd century emperors kept getting overthrown and killed, there was more than 20 emperors in about 50 years. All the civil wars wasted money and soldiers.

The economy was also bad. Taxes were really high, there was inflation because they put less silver in the coins, and trade was disrupted. Lots of farmers left their land and cities got smaller.

There was also pressure from outside. Germanic tribes like the Goths, Vandals and Franks were attacking the borders, partly because the Huns were pushing them west. Rome started using these same tribes as soldiers but they weren't always loyal.

In 395 the empire was split into east and west. The east had Constantinople and was richer and easier to defend, so the west was left weaker.

The Visigoths sacked Rome in 410 and the Vandals did it again in 455. Most people say it ended in 476 when Odoacer got rid of the last emperor Romulus Augustulus. The eastern half (the Byzantine Empire) kept going until 1453 though, which is kind of crazy.
