Short answer: a heat pump moves heat rather than making it. That's why it can deliver three or four units of heat for every unit of electricity it uses.

Here's how it works. A refrigerant absorbs heat from the outside air, even on cold days. A compressor squeezes that refrigerant, which raises its temperature. The hot refrigerant then releases its heat inside your home.

A few things affect how well it performs:
- The outdoor temperature. Efficiency drops as it gets colder.
- Insulation. A leaky house loses the heat as fast as the pump supplies it.
- Sizing. An undersized unit runs constantly and still struggles.

For example, in Brisbane's mild winters a reverse-cycle air conditioner is usually the cheapest way to heat a room. In short, it's efficient because it relocates heat instead of generating it.
